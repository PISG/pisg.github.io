What you are responsible for

pisg turns chat logs into a page about the people who wrote them. Running it is your decision and publishing the result is your act:

  • Have the right to the logs you feed it, and follow the rules of the network and channel they came from.
  • Obey the law where you and your users are. Nicknames, timestamps, quoted messages and behaviour summaries are personal information in Canada (PIPEDA, Quebec's Law 25), in the EEA and the UK (GDPR), and in many other places. If you publish them, the obligations are yours, not the project's: tell people, honour requests to be left out (<user nick="…" ignore="y">), and do not keep more than you need.
  • Do not use pisg to harass, profile or expose anyone. It is a toy for a channel to look at itself, not a surveillance tool.
  • Check before you publish. Random quotes and the URL list can carry more than you expect; ShowRandQuote and BadUrls exist for that.

The privacy policy has the practical checklist.
